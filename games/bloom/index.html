The garden year

  • Everything you need is in the cottage: tools on the rack, seeds on the shelf, compost in the sack, the watering can by the pump.
  • Clear weeds, withered plants and litter — they drag the garden's beauty down. Small weeds come out by hand; big ones need the hoe; brambles yield to the shears.
  • Water soaks a 3×3 patch. Plants in dry soil wilt, then wither. Refill at the well, the pump or the pond. Rain waters everything.
  • Compost feeds the soil: plants grow faster and recover. Everything you clear goes into the compost bin by the gate and slowly becomes new compost.
  • Prune tired plants with the shears to revive them. Deadhead blooming ones so they flourish.
  • Till grass with the hoe, then sow seeds. Mix species and plant them close together — blooming neighbours and variety make the garden lovelier.
  • As the garden grows more beautiful, new seeds and ornaments appear in the cottage, and Grandmother's journal reveals new pages.
  • Sleep in the bed after dusk to skip the night.
